Why you lose your way

Willpower has little to do with it. Alone, you lose your way.

It happens to almost everyone, even people who never put a foot wrong at work. The numbers bear it out.

  1. 1 in 2

    Knowing what to do isn't enough.

    Around half of the people who decide to be more active don't follow through. You know the theory. Putting it into practice on your own is another matter.

    Source: Rhodes and de Bruijn, British Journal of Health Psychology, 2013

  2. 66days

    A new habit takes time.

    More than two months on average, not twenty-one days. And missing a day does no harm, as long as you pick it up again.

    Source: Lally and colleagues, European Journal of Social Psychology, 2010

  3. 1 in 5

    Those who succeed stick with it.

    Only around one in five people keeps the weight off long term. Those who do check their progress regularly and eat the same way during the week and at weekends.

    Source: Wing and Phelan, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2005

That's why I give you the plan, then stay with you until the new habits become your normal way of living.
